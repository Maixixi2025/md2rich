ChatGPT Brand Mentions 2026: OpenAI's New Analytics Panel

OpenAI is testing the first first-party measurement of how often a brand appears in ChatGPT answers. It counts mentions and clicks — and it hides the one thing every publisher actually needs.

For two years, the measurement of AI answers was guesswork. Marketers ran prompts through a chatbot at scale, recorded what came back, and sold the resulting visibility estimate to clients who had no way to check it. Search consultant Glenn Gabe called the structural gap plainly in a July 2025 essay: unlike Google and Bing, there was no reporting from the AI tools themselves, and site owners needed it urgently.

That is finally starting to change. According to a November 9 report from Digiday and separate coverage from PPC Land, OpenAI is building a dashboard that will show verified businesses how often they appear in ChatGPT responses and how many clicks those appearances send back to their website. The feature sits inside a wider product called Business Hub, behind a banner inviting companies to claim their listing in ChatGPT.

The news is significant for anyone publishing content into AI answers. But the shape of the panel matters more than its existence, and the shape has a hole in it exactly where you would want one. Here is what the tool measures, what it withholds, and what you can actually do about it while you wait.

What OpenAI is actually building

The reporting traces back to a single source. Juozas Kaziukenas, founder of the e-commerce research firm Marketplace Pulse, posted a screenshot on LinkedIn on November 6, 2026 after spotting a banner for the unreleased feature. He said he had first seen traces of it in August 2026, which suggests the dashboard has been in front of a small group for months.

The banner asks businesses to Claim your business in ChatGPT. According to two sources with knowledge of the workings, a claimed business can track its mentions and keep its product and business information current. Claiming also unlocks what OpenAI calls a business agent, which answers customer questions using context the business supplies. Getting in requires proof of authority to represent the business; connecting a Shopify store is one way to do that.

The measurement part is deliberately simple. The tool records two events: impressions, defined as the number of times a business appeared in ChatGPT responses, and clicks on links to the business's website. Both are counted daily and displayed on a chart.

OpenAI has not announced the feature, has not confirmed a launch date, and declined to comment for the Digiday story. Everything above rests on one person's view of an interface that has not shipped. That caveat matters, but the shape of the product is worth studying now, because it is the same shape every major platform is converging on — and it is very different from the shape of a search console.

The measurement gap nobody can fill

Here is the part that should shape your expectations. The dashboard, as described, shows no prompt data, no user data, and no competitive benchmark or ranking. The charts cannot explain why mentions or clicks rise or fall.

That is not a small limitation. It is the difference between a thermometer and a diagnosis. A mention count tells you the patient has a fever. Prompt data tells you what the infection is. Without it, you are left in the position Gabe described in 2025 — watching a number move and guessing.

Gabe had published a twelve-feature wishlist for what he called an AI Search Console, or ASC. PPC Land compared that list against what has been reported about Business Hub:

Feature requested in 2025 Reported status
Impressions in AI answers Described (appearances in responses)
Clicks and engagement Clicks described; engagement not described
Trending over time Described (daily chart)
Prompts a site shows up for Explicitly excluded
Filtering prompts and landing pages Not described
Reporting by device and country Not described
Competitive ranking against rivals Not described

Removal tool by URL or path Not described
API access for bulk export Not described

Three of twelve, by the count of features actually described. Gabe had called an API essential for building the advanced reporting tools that agencies and consultants sell, and the reported tool has none.

A mention is not a citation

Definitions matter more than the chart, and this is where the reported metric diverges most sharply from everything publishers already know.

Kaziukenas described an impression as the number of times a business appeared in ChatGPT responses. Gabe defined an AI impression differently — as impressions in AI answers based on a site's content being referenced or quoted. Those are not the same event. A retailer can be named in an answer without any of its pages serving as a source. A publisher's article can be cited in an answer that is about somebody else's product entirely.

Whether the ChatGPT tool counts text mentions, product cards, citations, or sidebar links has not been described. And the counting rule alone can swing the numbers by nearly threefold. In a confidential OpenAI report made available to a small group of publishers, documented by Vincent Terrasi in Search Engine Journal in December 2025, one large publisher's top URL logged 185,000 distinct conversation impressions and 3,800 clicks in a month — a 2% click-through rate. Counting repeat appearances within the same conversations, the same URL showed 518,000 impressions and 4,400 clicks. Same page, same month, and the rate depends entirely on whether repeats count.

That report also suggests OpenAI has measured impressions and clicks for selected publishing partners since at least late 2025. The genuine novelty of Business Hub would be extending that access to ordinary businesses.

Three platforms, three partial answers

Business Hub does not exist in a vacuum. Google and Bing both shipped their own versions of this measurement, and each made a different trade-off. Understanding what each one chose to withhold tells you what to expect from OpenAI too.

Platform Reports Withholds
Google AI impressions (AI Overviews, AI Mode, Discover generative features), broken down by page, country, device, and date Clicks, click-through rate, and queries
Bing Total citations, average cited pages per day, grounding queries, page-level citation activity Clicks; grounding queries are a sample only
ChatGPT (reported) Brand mentions and clicks, daily, on a chart Prompts, user data, competitor rankings

Google added generative AI reports to Search Console on June 3, 2026 and extended them to every site as of August 31, 2026. They count impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode and can be broken down by country — but they show no clicks, no click-through rate, and no queries. Google also gave site owners a control to exclude their sites from AI Overviews and AI Mode, working at domain level, with page-level control scheduled for March 3, 2027.

Bing opened a public preview of AI Performance in Webmaster Tools on February 10, 2026, covering Copilot, AI summaries in Bing, and select partner integrations. Its launch metrics were total citations, average cited pages per day, grounding queries, and page-level citation activity. On March 23, 2026, it mapped grounding queries to the pages they cite, and in October it added a Topics Portfolio beta showing queries where other sites are cited — the competitive view that the reported ChatGPT tool reportedly lacks.

So: Google reports AI impressions without AI clicks or queries. Bing reports citations and sampled queries. ChatGPT, if it ships as described, would report mentions and clicks without prompts. None of the three has shipped the full set Gabe listed in 2025. Any vendor claiming to give you all of it is selling you a simulation of the thing you are trying to measure.

Crawling is the least-spoken-of part

For publishers weighing access, crawl data would answer a sharper question than a mention count. OpenAI revised its crawler documentation in December 2025 to state that robots.txt rules may not apply to ChatGPT-User, because its requests are initiated by users rather than by a crawler.

This is worth understanding precisely, because the four agents do different jobs. OpenAI's own documentation distinguishes them:

  • OAI-SearchBot — used to surface websites in ChatGPT's search features. Sites opted out of this agent will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links
  • OAI-AdsBot — visits landing pages submitted as ads to validate them against policy. It only visits pages submitted as ads, and its data is not used for training
  • GPTBot — crawls content that may be used for training OpenAI's generative AI foundation models
  • ChatGPT-User — used for certain user actions, including Custom GPTs and GPT Actions. It is not used for automatic crawling, and OpenAI states it is not used to determine whether content may appear in Search

OpenAI also notes that it can take roughly 24 hours for a robots.txt change to take effect. And blocking is a weaker opt-out than it looks: a BuzzStream study reported in April 2026 found that 82.4% of the top 50 news sites blocking OAI-SearchBot still appeared in citations. If you block the agent, you are likely still present in answers anyway — you just lose the ability to see it.

Why clicks are worth more than they look — and where they stop

The interest in a first-party click counter comes from how small AI referral traffic still is. Ahrefs research across 76,000 websites, published in February 2026, put ChatGPT at 0.21% of site traffic against nearly 40% for Google. In an analysis published in December 2025, Microsoft Clarity put AI referrals at under 1% of visitors across more than 1,200 publisher and news sites — though that traffic had grown 155% in eight months and converted to sign-ups at 1.66%, against 0.15% for organic search.

But a click counter inside ChatGPT measures only part of that effect. Similarweb data reported in June 2026 found that brands ChatGPT recommended were 2.5 times more likely to receive a visit within seven days, and that 55.9% of AI-influenced visits arrived through search rather than directly from the assistant. A user who reads about a brand in ChatGPT and then searches for it on Google produces no ChatGPT click at all. Standard analytics credit that later visit to search, so AI referral figures capture only the direct portion. A first-party count would firm up that portion without settling the rest.

There is a second reason first-party counts matter, and it is structural. The third-party visibility market depends on platforms tolerating simulated prompt traffic. Semrush is dropping Copilot from its tracking on November 9, 2026, citing changes to Microsoft's access requirements, cutting the number of platforms it tracks from nine to eight. The IAB said in August that only 16% of brands systematically track AI visibility. Tools that simulate prompts can be switched off at any time; first-party counts cannot. That is exactly the strength Kaziukenas pointed to — and exactly why the prompt-tracking tools would still be needed alongside the dashboard, not replaced by it.

2. Keep the name next to the claim

The research by Suganthan Mohanadasan, covered by PPC Land in August, found that only 3.1% of 3,554 pages ChatGPT retrieved earned a citation across 60 conversations on a single account. But brands that ChatGPT itself named in its internal search queries reached the final answer 68.9% of the time, against 2.1% for brands whose pages were fetched but never named in those queries. The lesson is uncomfortable: being fetched is nearly irrelevant. Being named is what correlates with appearing. So write your brand name into the sentence, not just the footer.

3. Publish the same sentence everywhere

AI answers draw from a wide, messy surface — the open web, social posts, forums, and platform-native content. If you write one definition in one place, you have one surface. If you write it in twelve places with identical wording, you have twelve chances to be the source, and the phrasing stays consistent enough for a model to attribute cleanly.

FAQ

What is OpenAI's Business Hub and when does it launch?

Business Hub is an unreleased OpenAI feature, still in testing as of November 2026, that would let verified businesses claim a listing inside ChatGPT and see how often they are mentioned in answers plus how many clicks those mentions send to their site. Both numbers are counted daily and plotted on a chart. The feature was first spotted in early form in August 2026 and surfaced publicly on November 6, 2026 through a screenshot posted by Juozas Kaziukenas, founder of the e-commerce research firm Marketplace Pulse. Claiming a business requires proof of authority to represent it, and connecting a Shopify store is one route. OpenAI has not confirmed a launch date.

Does the ChatGPT dashboard show which prompts produced a mention?

No. According to the reporting by Juozas Kaziukenas, the dashboard records impressions and clicks but shows no prompt data, no user data, and no competitive benchmark or ranking. That is the single most important limitation of the reported feature. Search consultant Glenn Gabe had published a twelve-feature wishlist for an AI Search Console in July 2025, and by press analysis the reported tool meets only three of them: impressions, clicks, and a trend line. Prompt data is explicitly missing and the remaining eight features have not been described.

How is a ChatGPT brand mention different from a Google or Bing citation?

A citation means one of your pages was used as the source of the answer. A mention, as described for Business Hub, means your business appeared in the response. Those are different events. A retailer can be named in an answer without any of its pages serving as a source, and a publisher article can be cited in an answer that is about a completely different product. Google and Bing report citations and how often content is used as a source. ChatGPT's reported panel would count appearances of the business, which is closer to brand visibility than to source attribution.

Do I need to change robots.txt to appear in ChatGPT answers?

You need OAI-SearchBot allowed if you want your site eligible for ChatGPT search results. OpenAI's own crawler documentation lists four distinct agents: OAI-SearchBot for search inclusion, OAI-AdsBot for validating pages submitted as ads, GPTBot for training, and ChatGPT-User for user-initiated fetches, which OpenAI states is not used to determine whether content may appear in Search. OpenAI also notes it can take about 24 hours for a robots.txt change to take effect. A BuzzStream study reported in April 2026 found that 82.4 percent of the top 50 news sites blocking OAI-SearchBot still appeared in citations, so blocking is a weak opt-out for brand mentions.

The verdict

The reported Business Hub dashboard is a real step, and the direction is right: first-party measurement is what the market has lacked since Gabe's essay. But the metric it exposes is a brand mention count, not a source attribution, and the panel deliberately withholds the prompts that would explain it. Three of twelve requested features, on the count of what has actually been described.

So the practical 2026 position is this: treat first-party AI analytics as something to check when it ships, not something to plan around. Meanwhile, do the things that are under your control and that pay off regardless of which dashboard you look at. Write the sentence you want quoted. Put your name next to it. Publish it identically everywhere. And keep your source format in a plain text file so that "everywhere" is a build step rather than a project.
